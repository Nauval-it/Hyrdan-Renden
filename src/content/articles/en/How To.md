      Cockroaches are among the most ancient and resilient insects on Earth.
      Belonging to the order Blattodea, these creatures have inhabited the
      planet for hundreds of millions of years, successfully surviving the mass
      extinction events that wiped out the dinosaurs. Their continuous presence
      today stands as a testament to their extraordinary evolutionary design and
      biological adaptability.


      From an anatomical perspective, the cockroach is built for survival in
      extreme conditions. Its flattened, dorsoventral body shape allows it to
      squeeze through remarkably tiny crevices and cracks to escape predators.
      Protected by a tough chitinous exoskeleton covered with a water-resistant
      waxy layer, the cockroach can endure significant physical pressure while
      preventing fatal moisture loss.


      The metabolic and physiological traits of cockroaches are both fascinating
      and surprising. As opportunistic omnivores, they can digest almost any
      organic matter, including food scraps, paper, glue, and decaying plant
      material. Furthermore, because they breathe through tiny lateral pores
      called spiracles rather than a mouth, a cockroach can survive for several
      weeks without its head, eventually dying only from starvation or
      dehydration.


      In natural ecosystems, cockroaches play a vital ecological role that is
      frequently overlooked. In forests and wild habitats, they act as essential
      decomposers by consuming dead vegetation and animal matter, breaking it
      down, and returning key nutrients back to the soil. Without such
      decomposers, the natural cycle of nutrient recycling would slow
      dramatically, disrupting the broader food chain.


      When they enter human dwellings, however, cockroaches transition from
      beneficial decomposers to hazardous pests. Their affinity for unsanitary
      environments—such as sewers, drains, and garbage bins—allows them to pick
      up and spread harmful bacteria, parasites, and pathogens. Food or surfaces
      contaminated by cockroaches can trigger digestive illnesses like typhoid
      and gastroenteritis, as well as severe allergic reactions and asthma in
      sensitive individuals.

      Preparation & Sanitation


      Eliminate Food Sources: Store all food in airtight containers, wipe down
      countertops, sweep up crumbs, and never leave dirty dishes in the sink
      overnight.


      Manage Waste: Use garbage cans with tight-fitting lids and empty household
      trash every evening.


      Remove Water Access: Fix leaking pipes and dry out sinks and bathroom
      areas, as cockroaches need moisture to survive.
    right: >-
      Active Extermination


      Deploy Gel Baits: Place commercial cockroach bait gels in dark corners,
      under sinks, and behind appliances so insects carry the slow-acting poison
      back to infect the nest.


      Apply Insecticide Sprays: Spray targeted contact insecticides directly
      into cracks, crevices, and baseboards where cockroaches hide.


      Set Sticky Traps: Position glue boards along walls and dark pathways to
      catch roaming cockroaches and monitor infestation levels.
  - type: paragraph
    text: >-
      Long-Term Prevention


      Seal Entry Points: Use caulk or sealant to fill gaps in walls, tile
      crevices, and spaces around plumbing pipes or door frames.


      Cover Drains: Install mesh screens or backflow-preventing drain covers in
      sinks and shower outlets to stop pests from crawling up from sewers.


      Clean Deep Regularly: Vacuum behind heavy furniture and appliances
      regularly to destroy hidden egg cases (oothecae) before they hatch.
